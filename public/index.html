The format

A competition with money at every gate.

Not a course. Not a demo day with certificates. A published bar, real cash at each checkpoint, and a field that narrows until the number is hit.

01

Apply to a seat

Ten minutes: a form and a sixty second video. Any LAU student, or an LAU graduate from the last three years; more universities as founding members join. Teams of two or three, and one of you must be able to build the thing.

02

Five teams enter

$1,000 lands on day one. So do lab and rig time, software licences, and the introductions to utilities and operators you cannot get on your own.

03

Clear the checkpoints

Weeks 2, 6, and 10. The expert panel scores your evidence against the published bar. Clear it and more money lands. Miss it and your run ends there, with everything already paid.

04

Measurement day

Week 12, in public, in front of investors and engineers. Your system hits the published number or it does not. If it does, you take $12,000.

05

Go raise

Finishers leave with a working system, verified evidence, and applications written for the programmes that write the first real cheque.

Five teams. $1,000 each on day one. The bar for every checkpoint is already published.

Five seats

Three verticals. Two open seats.

You apply to a seat, not to a theme. Each vertical is a territory where a measurable win changes a real industry. What exactly you build inside it is yours; the number you will be judged on is fixed with the expert panel before week one.

Seat 01 · Energy

Make real infrastructure cheaper to run

Cut what a facility spends on power and prove the saving on live load, over weeks, without slowing anything down.

Seat 02 · Water

Do more with less hardware

Find loss faster. Locate leaks with a fraction of the sensors. Cut waste by an amount a utility can verify.

Seat 03 · Compute

Same work, a fraction of the power

Run real workloads on less silicon and less energy, at the same accuracy, measured against an off-the-shelf baseline.

Seats 04 · 05 · Open

Bring your own vertical

Two of the five seats are open to any field. Health, agriculture, logistics, materials, finance: it qualifies the moment it carries a number we can measure, a test we can run, and a deadline we can hold you to.

The contract

One number. One test. One deadline. Fixed with the expert panel before week one, published before you start, and judged exactly as written. If it cannot be measured, it cannot compete.

The standard

Advancement is earned.
Never given.

The bar is public before week one

Every checkpoint number is published before the cohort starts and does not move after. Reviewers score evidence against it: measurements, logs, working systems. Not decks, not effort, not potential. The complete rule set ships with the application as a versioned document.

Judged by working experts

Investors, operators, and engineers active in each vertical score every checkpoint. Reviewer names are published when cohort one is seated. Any reviewer can request an introduction to any team at any point, including teams that were cut. A team cut at week six with three investors asking to talk has not failed at anything.

Nobody has to advance

If no team clears a checkpoint, no team advances. The unclaimed money rolls into the next cohort and the bar stays exactly where it was. Winning here has to mean something, so it is allowed to mean nothing this time.

No equity. Ever.

Not a share, not a warrant, not a right to invest later. You own everything you make, and applying signs nothing away.

No mentorship you did not ask for

We give you the number, the money, and the lab. No curriculum, no workshops, no fireside chats. Ninety minutes a week, and the rest of your time is yours.

Getting cut costs nothing

Money paid is money kept, equipment included. Nothing gets clawed back, and you can enter the next cohort with the same idea.

Alumni
